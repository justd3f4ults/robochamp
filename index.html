History

Through the Centuries

From royal retreat to World Heritage Site — the story of Qasr Amra.

723 – 743 AD · 105 AH
Built by Walid ibn Yazid, future Umayyad Caliph Walid II, as a royal desert retreat and bathhouse along the ancient caravan route east of Amman.
8th Century
The castle's walls and ceilings are adorned with extraordinary frescoes — hunting scenes, celestial maps, the famous "Six Kings" painting, and one of the earliest known star maps on a dome.
1898
Rediscovered by Austrian explorer Alois Musil, who documented the stunning frescoes for the world. The site had been hidden by desert sands for centuries.
1970s – 1990s
Major restoration campaigns by Spanish and Italian teams reveal the vibrant original colors of the frescoes hidden beneath centuries of grime and shellac.
1985
Designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site — recognized as a masterpiece of early Islamic art and a unique testament to the Umayyad civilization.
Today
One of Jordan's most visited archaeological sites, Qasr Amra continues to captivate visitors with its remarkably preserved architecture and frescoes — still standing after 13 centuries.
